## KC022
video:
### Explanation
When you describe hobbies, frequency adverbs follow the same rules as in routines: **before the main verb** (I **often** go hiking), **after be** (She is **rarely** at home on Saturdays), and after an auxiliary (I don't **usually** paint). Expressions like **at weekends** or **twice a month** go at the end.
### Examples
- My cousin **sometimes** plays chess online.
- We are **always** busy on match days.
- I go climbing **every other weekend**.
### Common mistake
- ~~I play often the drums.~~ → I **often play** the drums.
- ~~She goes to the gym usually.~~ (possible in speech) → She **usually goes** to the gym.
### Try it
- Add "hardly ever": "I watch football." => I hardly ever watch football.
- Fix: "We on Sundays go fishing." => We go fishing on Sundays.

## KC023
video:
### Explanation
Some verbs are followed by **-ing** (a gerund), others by **to + verb** (an infinitive). **Enjoy, mind, finish, avoid** take -ing. **Want, hope, decide, plan** take to-infinitive. **Like, love, hate, prefer** can take either with little difference in meaning. **Would like / would prefer** always take **to**.
### Examples
- I **enjoy painting** landscapes.
- She **decided to join** a choir.
- I'**d like to try** surfing one day.
### Common mistake
- ~~I enjoy to swim.~~ → I enjoy **swimming**.
- ~~I would like going.~~ → I would like **to go**.
### Try it
- Complete: "He hopes ___ (run) a marathon." => to run
- Complete: "They avoid ___ (cycle) in the rain." => cycling

## KC024
video:
### Explanation
Time prepositions: **at** for clock times and short points (at 8 pm, at noon, at the weekend in British English); **on** for days and dates (on Friday, on 12 May, on my birthday); **in** for months, years, seasons and parts of the day (in July, in 2025, in summer, in the evening).
### Examples
- The concert starts **at** half past seven.
- The match is **on** Sunday afternoon.
- The festival takes place **in** August.
### Common mistake
- ~~in Monday~~ → **on** Monday; ~~on the morning~~ → **in** the morning (but **on** Monday morning).
### Try it
- Complete: "The film begins ___ 9:15." => at
- Complete: "We go skiing ___ winter." => in

## KC044
video:
### Explanation
Leisure activities often use **go + -ing** for movement (go hiking, go camping, go cycling), **play** for games with rules or balls (play chess, play volleyball) and **do** for martial arts or general activities (do yoga, do karate). Creative hobbies have their own nouns: **photography**, **pottery**, **knitting**, **gardening**.
### Examples
- We **go camping** every summer.
- He **does** judo twice a week.
- **Pottery** is relaxing and creative.
### Common mistake
- ~~play yoga~~ → **do** yoga; ~~do football~~ → **play** football.
### Try it
- Which verb: "___ climbing" (an outdoor activity)? => go
- Which verb: "___ karate"? => do

## KC045
video:
### Explanation
Each sport has its own **equipment** and **place**. Tennis: racket, court. Golf: club, course. Swimming: goggles, pool. Football: boots, pitch. Skiing: skis, poles, slope. Boxing: gloves, ring. Learn them in sets: sport + equipment + venue.
### Examples
- You need a **racket** to play badminton on a **court**.
- Golfers hit the ball with a **club** on a **course**.
- Wear **goggles** in the **pool**.
### Common mistake
- ~~football court~~ → football **pitch** (court is for tennis, basketball, badminton).
### Try it
- Where do people ski? => on a slope
- What do boxers wear on their hands? => gloves

## KC046
video:
### Explanation
Event vocabulary: a **venue** is the place where an event happens; an **exhibition** shows art or objects; a **gig** is an informal concert; a **performance** is a show; the **audience** watches; the **box office** sells tickets; **sold out** means no tickets are left; **doors open** is the time you can enter.
### Examples
- The **venue** holds 2,000 people.
- The photography **exhibition** runs until June.
- The show is **sold out**, so we can't go.
### Common mistake
- Confusing **audience** (people watching) with **public** (people in general).
### Try it
- The website says "No tickets left". Which phrase means the same? => sold out
- What do we call the people watching a show? => the audience

## KC067
video:
### Explanation
Invitations and flyers give key details in short form: **what** (event), **when** (date, start time, **doors open**), **where** (venue, address) and **extras** (RSVP by, dress code, tickets). "Doors open 19:00, show 20:00" means you can enter at 7 but the show begins at 8.
### Examples
- "Doors 18:30 / Start 19:15 / Hall B" → enter at 18:30, start at 19:15.
- "**RSVP** by 10 May" → reply before 10 May.
- "Free entry, donations welcome" → no ticket needed.
### Common mistake
- Taking "doors open" as the start time of the event.
### Try it
- "Doors 19:30, band on stage 20:30." When does the music start? => 20:30
- "RSVP to Mia by Friday." What must guests do? => Reply to Mia by Friday

## KC068
video:
### Explanation
Club and community-centre timetables list **activity**, **day**, **time**, **room** and sometimes **level** (beginners, advanced) or **age group**. To find a slot, filter by your conditions first (day you are free, your level), then read the time and room.
### Examples
- "Tue 18:00 Beginners' Spanish, Room 3" → for beginners, Tuesday evening.
- "Sat 10:00 Junior swim (8-12 yrs)" → children only.
- "Thu 19:30 Yoga (all levels)" → anyone can join.
### Common mistake
- Missing a condition such as level or age: an "Advanced" class may not be right for a beginner.
### Try it
- "Mon 17:00 Kids' art (6-10). Wed 19:00 Adult art." You are 30. Which day? => Wednesday
- "Fri 20:00 Badminton, Court 2." Where is badminton? => Court 2

## KC069
video:
### Explanation
Tutorials are **step-by-step** guides. Numbers or sequence words show the order; **imperatives** tell you what to do (Cut, Glue, Mix). Look for **materials** at the start, **warnings** (Do not..., Make sure...) and **checkpoints** (until..., when it turns...).
### Examples
- "1. Sand the wood. 2. Apply the first coat of paint. 3. Leave to dry **for two hours**."
- "**Make sure** the glue is dry before you turn it over."
- "Stir **until** the mixture is smooth."
### Common mistake
- Skipping the materials list or waiting times, so the project fails halfway.
### Try it
- "Mix the paint. Then let it dry overnight. Finally, add the second colour." What happens after the paint dries? => You add the second colour
- "Do not water the seeds for the first day." What should you avoid on day one? => Watering the seeds

## KC091
video:
### Explanation
A paragraph about your hobby should say **what** you do, **how often / since when**, **why** you enjoy it and an **example**. Use the present simple for habits, the present perfect for duration (I **have played** the violin **for** six years) and preference verbs with the correct form (I **love playing**...).
### Examples
- I **have been** a keen photographer **since** 2020.
- I usually take pictures of street life **because** I like capturing ordinary moments.
- Last month I **won** a small local competition.
### Common mistake
- Listing hobbies without reasons or examples ("I like sport. I like music.").
- ~~I play guitar since five years.~~ → I **have played** the guitar **for** five years.
### Try it
- Add a reason: "I enjoy gardening." => I enjoy gardening because it helps me relax after work.
- Correct: "I do photography since 2018." => I have done photography since 2018.

## KC092
video:
### Explanation
An informal invitation includes the **occasion**, **date and time**, **place**, **what to bring** and **how to reply**. Use friendly language: "**Would you like to come**...?", "**I'm having** a barbecue on...", "**Let me know** by Friday if you can make it."
### Examples
- **I'm having** a small birthday party **on** Saturday 14th **at** 7 pm.
- It's **at** my flat, 22 Hill Road.
- **Bring** something to drink, and **let me know** if you can come!
### Common mistake
- Forgetting the time or address, so guests have to ask.
- Too formal for friends: "I hereby invite you..." → **Would you like to come** to...?
### Try it
- Invite a friend to a picnic on Sunday at noon in Central Park. => Would you like to come to a picnic in Central Park on Sunday at noon?
- Which phrase asks for a reply? => Let me know if you can come.

## KC093
video:
### Explanation
A short social post summarises an activity in **two to four sentences**: what you did (past simple), where and with whom, one highlight, and a feeling. Informal tone is fine, but keep the grammar correct and the order clear.
### Examples
- Spent Saturday kayaking on the lake with Jo and Max.
- The highlight **was** seeing a heron up close!
- Tired but happy - **can't wait** to go again.
### Common mistake
- Using the present for a finished weekend: ~~I go to the beach last Sunday.~~ → I **went** to the beach last Sunday.
### Try it
- Write a one-line post: you visited a market with your sister and bought cheese. => Went to the market with my sister and bought some amazing cheese!
- Correct: "We see a great band on Friday." (last Friday) => We saw a great band on Friday.
