## KC019
video:
### Explanation
Comparatives compare two things. One-syllable adjectives add **-er** (cheap → cheaper); a final consonant after one vowel doubles (big → bigger); -y becomes **-ier** (easy → easier). Adjectives of two or more syllables usually take **more** (more expensive, more reliable). Irregular: good → **better**, bad → **worse**, far → **further**.
### Examples
- This laptop is **lighter than** the old one.
- The blue jacket is **more comfortable**.
- Online prices are often **lower than** in shops.
### Common mistake
- ~~more cheaper~~ → **cheaper**.
- ~~expensiver~~ → **more expensive**.
### Try it
- Complete: "The new phone is ___ (thin) than the last model." => thinner
- Complete: "This brand is ___ (reliable) than that one." => more reliable

## KC020
video:
### Explanation
Superlatives pick out **one** item from a group. Use **the** + **-est** for short adjectives (the cheapest) and **the most** for long ones (the most popular). Irregular: **the best**, **the worst**. Often followed by **in** + place or **of** + group.
### Examples
- This is **the cheapest** charger **in** the shop.
- The red model is **the most popular of** the three.
- That was **the worst** purchase I've made.
### Common mistake
- Forgetting "the": ~~It is cheapest one.~~ → It is **the cheapest** one.
- ~~the most fastest~~ → **the fastest**.
### Try it
- Complete: "It's ___ (large) TV we sell." => the largest
- Complete: "This is ___ (useful) app on my phone." => the most useful

## KC021
video:
### Explanation
Demonstratives point to things. **This** (singular) and **these** (plural) are **near** the speaker; **that** (singular) and **those** (plural) are **further away**. They must match the noun: this shirt / these shirts, that bag / those bags.
### Examples
- How much is **this** scarf (in my hand)?
- I'd like to try **those** boots in the window.
- **These** apples look fresh.
### Common mistake
- ~~These shoe is too small.~~ → **This** shoe / **These** shoes.
- ~~Those is my bag.~~ → **That** is my bag.
### Try it
- Complete (plural, near): "Are ___ gloves on sale?" => these
- Complete (singular, far): "Can you show me ___ watch on the top shelf?" => that

## KC041
video:
### Explanation
Shopping words: the **cashier** takes payment at the **checkout** or **till**; a **receipt** proves you paid; a **refund** gives you your money back; an **exchange** swaps an item for another; a **discount** reduces the price; something **on sale** is cheaper for a limited time; a **voucher** can be used instead of money.
### Examples
- Keep your **receipt** in case you need a **refund**.
- There is a 20% **discount** on all shoes.
- Can I **exchange** this for a larger size?
### Common mistake
- "Refund" is the money back; "**return**" is bringing the item back.
### Try it
- You bring a shirt back and get your money. What do you get? => a refund
- What is a paper or code you can spend like money in a shop? => a voucher

## KC042
video:
### Explanation
Payment words: pay **in cash** or **by card** (credit or debit); **contactless** means tapping the card or phone; a **PIN** is the secret number; **instalments** split a price into several payments; **change** is the money you get back when you pay too much; **interest** is the extra cost of borrowing.
### Examples
- Can I pay **by card**?
- The sofa can be paid in six monthly **instalments**.
- Here is your **change**: two euros.
### Common mistake
- ~~pay with cash~~ is possible, but **pay in cash** is more common; ~~pay by cash~~ is wrong.
- Confusing **debit** (money from your account now) with **credit** (borrowed, paid later).
### Try it
- You tap your card on the machine. What kind of payment is it? => contactless
- What is the secret number for your card? => a PIN

## KC043
video:
### Explanation
Product descriptions give **dimensions** (height, width, depth, length, weight) and **materials** (cotton, wool, leather, plastic, stainless steel, wooden). Measurements use **cm**, **m**, **kg** and the form "50 cm **wide**", "1.2 kg **in weight**". Adjectives from materials usually have no ending: a **cotton** shirt, a **leather** belt, but a **wooden** table, a **woollen** hat.
### Examples
- The desk is 120 cm **wide** and 60 cm **deep**.
- A **waterproof** jacket keeps you dry.
- These mugs are made of **ceramic**.
### Common mistake
- ~~a wood table~~ → a **wooden** table; ~~a leathery bag~~ → a **leather** bag.
### Try it
- Complete: "The shelf is 30 cm ___." (from front to back) => deep
- Which material word fits: "a ___ spoon" (made of steel)? => steel / stainless steel

## KC064
video:
### Explanation
Shop signs state offers in different ways. "**20% off**" = pay 80%; "**Half price**" = 50% off; "**Buy one, get one free**" = two for the price of one; "**3 for 2**" = take three, pay for two; "**Up to 50% off**" = some items, not all. Read the small print for conditions (selected items, until Sunday).
### Examples
- A €40 bag with **25% off** costs €30.
- "**3 for 2** on books": three €10 books cost €20.
- "**Up to** 70% off" → maybe only a few items are 70% off.
### Common mistake
- Reading "up to" as "all items"; it means "the biggest discount is".
### Try it
- A €60 coat is 30% off. What do you pay? => €42
- "Buy one, get one free" on €5 candles. You take two. What do you pay? => €5

## KC065
video:
### Explanation
Return policies say **what** can be returned, **when** (within 14 / 30 days), **in what condition** (unused, with tags, in original packaging), **with what proof** (receipt) and **what you get** (refund, exchange, store credit). Words like **except**, **only**, **must** and **non-refundable** mark limits.
### Examples
- "Returns accepted **within 30 days** with **proof of purchase**."
- "Sale items can be **exchanged only**." → no money back.
- "Underwear is **non-refundable**." → cannot be returned for money.
### Common mistake
- Missing exceptions: a general "30-day returns" rule may not apply to sale or personal items.
### Try it
- "Refunds within 14 days with receipt; otherwise store credit." You have no receipt. What do you get? => store credit
- "Opened software cannot be returned." Can you return a game you opened? => No

## KC066
video:
### Explanation
Comparison tables put products in **columns** and features in **rows** (price, size, battery, warranty). To answer a question, find the **feature row** first, then compare across. Watch the **units** (GB vs TB, months vs years) and symbols (✓ = yes, - = no).
### Examples
- Battery: A = 10 h, B = 14 h → B lasts longer.
- Warranty: A = 1 year, B = 24 months → B has the longer warranty.
- Weight: A = 1.4 kg, B = 900 g → B is lighter.
### Common mistake
- Comparing numbers with different units without converting (900 g is less than 1.4 kg).
### Try it
- Storage: X = 512 GB, Y = 1 TB. Which stores more? => Y
- Price: Model A €299, Model B €349. Which is cheaper and by how much? => Model A, by €50

## KC088
video:
### Explanation
A return or refund request should give the **order details** (order number, date), the **problem** (damaged, faulty, wrong size), and the **outcome you want** (refund, replacement). Keep it polite and factual: "**I am writing to request** a refund for...", "**Unfortunately**, the item...", "**I would appreciate** a replacement".
### Examples
- **I am writing about** order 58213, delivered on 3 March.
- **Unfortunately**, the kettle stopped working after two days.
- **I would therefore like to request** a full refund.
### Common mistake
- Being angry or vague: "Your product is rubbish!" → describe the defect and the solution you want.
### Try it
- Write the problem sentence: lamp arrived with a broken shade. => Unfortunately, the lamp arrived with a broken shade.
- Complete: "I would like to ___ a replacement." => request

## KC089
video:
### Explanation
A comparison summary describes **two items feature by feature** and ends with a **recommendation**. Use comparatives, **while / whereas** for contrast, and **both** for similarities. Organise either item by item or feature by feature, and keep the same order throughout.
### Examples
- **Both** headphones are wireless.
- The Alpha is **lighter**, **whereas** the Beta has **better** sound.
- **For travelling**, I would choose the Alpha.
### Common mistake
- Listing features of each item separately with no direct comparison.
- Forgetting the conclusion, so the reader does not know which to choose.
### Try it
- Join: "Bag A has two pockets. Bag B has five pockets." (whereas) => Bag A has two pockets, whereas Bag B has five.
- Write a similarity: both chairs are adjustable. => Both chairs are adjustable.

## KC090
video:
### Explanation
An expense summary lists **date**, **item**, **category** and **amount**, and ends with a **total**. Write amounts consistently (€12.50, not "twelve and a half euros"), use the past simple for what was spent, and say clearly if anything is **over budget**.
### Examples
- 4 May - Train ticket (travel) - €23.80
- Total spent in May: **€412.30**; budget **€400**, so **€12.30 over budget**.
- I **spent** €56 on groceries this week.
### Common mistake
- Mixing number formats (12,5 / 12.50 / twelve) in one log.
- ~~I spend €20 yesterday.~~ → I **spent** €20 yesterday.
### Try it
- Write as a log line: on 2 June you paid €9.90 for a cinema ticket. => 2 June - Cinema ticket (leisure) - €9.90
- Budget €150, spent €162. How do you describe it? => €12 over budget
