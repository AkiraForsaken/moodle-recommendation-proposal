## KC009
video:
### Explanation
Prepositions of place describe where something is in relation to a landmark. **Next to** = beside, touching or very close; **between** = with one thing on each side; **opposite** = facing it, often across a street; **in front of / behind**; **on the corner of**; **near** = not far away.
### Examples
- The café is **next to** the cinema.
- The bus stop is **opposite** the hospital.
- There is a cash machine **on the corner of** King Street and Bell Road.
### Common mistake
- ~~The hotel is between the park.~~ → "between" needs **two** things: between the park **and** the river.
- ~~in the corner of the street~~ → **on** the corner (outside); **in** the corner of a room (inside).
### Try it
- Complete: "The library is ___ the school and the bank." => between
- Complete: "The museum is ___ the station; you can see it as you walk out." => opposite

## KC010
video:
### Explanation
Prepositions of movement describe a route. **Across** = from one side to the other of a flat area (a road, a square); **through** = inside something from one end to the other (a park, a tunnel); **past** = going by something without stopping; **along** = following a line (a river, a street); **over / under**; **into / out of**.
### Examples
- Walk **through** the market and you will reach the square.
- Drive **past** the petrol station.
- We cycled **along** the coast.
### Common mistake
- ~~Go through the street~~ (to the other side) → Go **across** the street.
- ~~Walk across the forest~~ (inside it) → Walk **through** the forest.
### Try it
- Complete: "Swim ___ the lake to the other side." => across
- Complete: "Keep walking ___ the canal until you see the lock." => along

## KC011
video:
### Explanation
Directions use the **imperative**: the base verb with no subject. Common verbs: **turn** left/right, **go / keep going** straight on, **take** the first street, **cross** the road, **follow** the signs. For a negative instruction use **Don't** + verb.
### Examples
- **Follow** the signs to the harbour.
- **Keep going** until the end of the road.
- **Don't take** the motorway; it is very busy.
### Common mistake
- Adding a subject: ~~You turn right and you go...~~ is possible in speech, but a clear written direction is: **Turn** right, then **go**...
- ~~No turn left here.~~ → **Don't turn** left here.
### Try it
- Make an instruction: "you / cross / the bridge" => Cross the bridge.
- Make a negative instruction: "you / park / here" => Don't park here.

## KC012
video:
### Explanation
To say which turning, combine an **ordinal number** (first, second, third) with a direction: "Take the **second left**", "Turn **right** at the **third** traffic lights". Count only the streets on the side you are turning to.
### Examples
- Take the **first right** after the roundabout.
- Turn left at the **second** set of lights.
- It is the **third** house on the left.
### Common mistake
- ~~Take the two left.~~ → Take the **second** left.
- Counting streets on both sides; count only those on the turning side.
### Try it
- Complete: "Take the ___ (3) right." => third
- You pass one left turning and take the next. Which is it? => the second left

## KC033
video:
### Explanation
Transport hubs have special words. At an airport: **check-in desk**, **security**, **departure lounge**, **gate**, **baggage reclaim**, **arrivals hall**. At a station: **platform**, **ticket office**, **concourse**. At a port: **harbour**, **pier**, **ferry terminal**.
### Examples
- Collect your suitcase at **baggage reclaim**.
- Our friends will wait for us in the **arrivals hall**.
- The ferry leaves from **Pier** 4.
### Common mistake
- Confusing **gate** (where you board a plane) with **terminal** (the whole building).
### Try it
- Where do you collect your luggage after a flight? => baggage reclaim
- Where do you wait for a train? => on the platform

## KC034
video:
### Explanation
Ticket words: a **single** (one-way) ticket goes one way; a **return** (round-trip) ticket goes there and back; the **fare** is the price of the journey; a **booking reference** identifies a reservation; a **boarding pass** lets you board a plane; **economy / business class**; a **seat reservation** guarantees a seat.
### Examples
- How much is the **fare** to the airport?
- Please keep your **booking reference** ready.
- I'd like a **window seat**, please.
### Common mistake
- ~~a back ticket~~ → a **return** ticket.
- Calling the price of a ticket "the cost of the trip ticket"; use **fare**.
### Try it
- What do you call a ticket that only goes one way? => a single (one-way) ticket
- Which code do you give to find your reservation? => the booking reference

## KC055
video:
### Explanation
Service alerts describe a **problem** (closed, delayed, cancelled), the **affected part** (a line, a stop, a time) and an **alternative**. To choose a route, check whether your stop is in the affected part and then follow the alternative exactly.
### Examples
- "Line 2 closed between Bay and Hill. Use shuttle buses." → If you travel between Bay and Hill, take the shuttle.
- "Stop 14 out of use. Next stop: Mill Lane." → Walk to Mill Lane.
- "The 17:05 is cancelled; the 17:35 will stop at all stations." → Take the 17:35.
### Common mistake
- Using the alternative even when your journey is **not** affected; check the affected section first.
### Try it
- "Tram 6 suspended between Zoo and Lake. Use Bus 30." You go from Zoo to Lake. What do you take? => Bus 30
- "Platform 4 closed. Trains to Dover leave from Platform 6." Where do you wait for Dover? => Platform 6

## KC056
video:
### Explanation
Departure boards are tables: **flight/train number, destination, time, gate/platform, status**. Find your **destination** first, then read across the same row. Status words: **On time**, **Boarding**, **Gate closing** (hurry), **Delayed**, **Cancelled**, **Departed**.
### Examples
- "KL1004 Amsterdam 11:30 Gate D3 On time" → Amsterdam leaves on time from D3.
- "Platform 9 15:42 Leeds Delayed 15:58" → The Leeds train now leaves at 15:58.
- "Gate closing" → Go to the gate immediately.
### Common mistake
- Reading the gate from the row above or below; keep your finger on the destination's row.
### Try it
- "EZY22 Madrid 07:05 Gate B9 Boarding". Which gate? => B9
- "Platform 3 12:10 Bath Cancelled". Can you take the 12:10 to Bath? => No, it is cancelled

## KC057
video:
### Explanation
A map **legend** (key) explains the symbols. Common symbols: **i** = tourist information, **P** = parking, a cross or "H" = hospital, a bed = hotel, a cup = café, a plane = airport. Lines show transport routes; colours separate lines, and dashed lines often mean "planned" or "seasonal".
### Examples
- A **green line** marked "M2" is metro line 2.
- A **WC** symbol shows public toilets.
- A **bus** symbol with a number shows a bus stop for that route.
### Common mistake
- Guessing a symbol instead of checking the legend; maps from different cities can use different symbols.
### Try it
- On most maps, what does a "P" mean? => parking
- A dashed line is labelled "summer only". What does it mean? => The route runs only in summer

## KC079
video:
### Explanation
Written directions should be **step by step**: one action per sentence or clause, in the right order, with **landmarks** so the reader can check progress. Use sequence words (**First, Then, When you reach..., After that**) and finish with where the destination is.
### Examples
- **First**, leave the car park and turn left.
- **When you reach** the fountain, turn right.
- The office is the **blue building on your left**.
### Common mistake
- Giving all steps in one long sentence with no landmarks, so the reader gets lost.
- Mixing up left and right after changing direction.
### Try it
- Put in order: "Then turn left. / The shop is on your right. / First, cross the square." => First, cross the square. Then turn left. The shop is on your right.
- Add a landmark: "Turn right." => Turn right at the post office.

## KC080
video:
### Explanation
An itinerary lists a trip **day by day** in time order. Use clear headings (**Day 1**, **Monday 4 June**), times (**09:00**), and time prepositions: **at** + clock time, **on** + day or date, **in** + part of the day. Keep each line short: time, activity, place.
### Examples
- **Day 2** - 08:30 breakfast at the hotel; 10:00 boat tour.
- **On** Friday **at** 14:00 we take the train to Nice.
- **In** the evening, dinner in the old town.
### Common mistake
- ~~In Monday~~ → **On** Monday; ~~at the afternoon~~ → **in** the afternoon.
- Listing activities out of time order.
### Try it
- Complete: "___ Saturday ___ 7 pm we go to the theatre." => On ... at
- Complete: "___ the morning we visit the market." => In

## KC081
video:
### Explanation
A written enquiry to a hotel should be polite and precise. Useful frames: "**Could you tell me whether**...", "**I would like to know if**...", "**I would be grateful if you could**...". Include the **dates**, the **room type** and the **specific question** (parking, late arrival, accessibility).
### Examples
- **Could you tell me whether** the room has a balcony?
- **I would like to know if** you offer airport transfers.
- **I would be grateful if you could** confirm my booking for 2-5 August.
### Common mistake
- Word order in indirect questions: ~~Could you tell me is parking free?~~ → Could you tell me **whether parking is** free?
### Try it
- Make indirect: "Is there a lift?" => Could you tell me whether there is a lift?
- Complete: "I would be grateful if you ___ (send) me a price list." => could send
