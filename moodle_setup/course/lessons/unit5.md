## KC016
video:
### Explanation
Quantifiers depend on whether a noun is **countable** (an egg, two eggs) or **uncountable** (rice, milk, bread). **Many / a few / few** go with plural countable nouns; **much / a little / little** go with uncountable nouns. **Some** is common in positive sentences and offers; **any** in questions and negatives. **A lot of** works with both.
### Examples
- There are **a few** olives left.
- We don't have **much** butter.
- Would you like **some** soup? / Is there **any** sugar?
### Common mistake
- ~~How many water do you drink?~~ → How **much** water...
- ~~I'd like an information about the menu.~~ → information is uncountable: **some information**.
### Try it
- Complete: "How ___ apples are in the bowl?" => many
- Complete: "There isn't ___ cheese in the fridge." => any / much

## KC017
video:
### Explanation
When ordering, polite forms sound friendlier than "I want". Use **I'd like** + noun (I'd like the salad), **I'll have** + noun (decision at the table), **Could I have...?** or **Can I get...?** (informal). The server may ask **Would you like...?** to offer something.
### Examples
- **I'd like** a table for four, please.
- **I'll have** the vegetable curry.
- **Could I have** some more water, please?
### Common mistake
- ~~I would like to the fish.~~ → I'd like **the fish** (noun) / I'd like **to order** the fish (verb).
- "Give me a coffee." can sound rude; say **Could I have** a coffee, please?
### Try it
- Make polite: "I want the pasta." => I'd like the pasta, please.
- What does the server say to offer dessert? => Would you like a dessert?

## KC018
video:
### Explanation
To compare food, use comparative adjectives. Short adjectives add **-er** (sweet → sweeter, hot → hotter); adjectives ending in -y change to **-ier** (spicy → spicier); longer adjectives use **more** (more delicious). Irregular: **good → better**, **bad → worse**. Use **than** before the second item.
### Examples
- This sauce is **spicier than** the one at home.
- The large portion is **bigger** but not **better**.
- The soup is **more filling than** the salad.
### Common mistake
- ~~more tastier~~ → **tastier** (never both -er and more).
- ~~gooder~~ → **better**; ~~than~~ forgotten: "This tea is stronger **than** that one."
### Try it
- Complete: "The cake is ___ (sweet) than the pie." => sweeter
- Complete: "Today's special is ___ (good) than yesterday's." => better

## KC038
video:
### Explanation
Cooking words describe how food is prepared: **grilled** (cooked over direct heat), **roasted** (cooked in an oven, often meat or vegetables), **steamed** (cooked over boiling water), **fried / deep-fried** (cooked in oil), **boiled** (in boiling water), **baked** (bread, cakes, potatoes in an oven), **raw** (not cooked).
### Examples
- **Steamed** rice with vegetables.
- **Baked** potatoes with cheese.
- Sushi often contains **raw** fish.
### Common mistake
- Using "fried" for food cooked in water; that is **boiled**.
- "Cooked" is general; menus prefer the exact method (grilled, roasted).
### Try it
- Food cooked over boiling water without touching it is... => steamed
- Bread and cakes are usually... => baked

## KC039
video:
### Explanation
Restaurant service words: the **server** (waiter/waitress) takes your order; the **menu** lists dishes; a **starter**, **main course** and **dessert** are the parts of a meal; the **bill** (US: check) shows what you owe; a **tip** or **gratuity** is extra money for service; a **service charge** is added to the bill by the restaurant.
### Examples
- Could we have the **bill**, please?
- Is the **service charge** included?
- We left a 10% **tip**.
### Common mistake
- Asking for the "receipt" before paying; first you ask for the **bill**, then you get a **receipt** as proof of payment.
### Try it
- What do you call extra money you give for good service? => a tip (gratuity)
- What is the first, small course of a meal? => a starter

## KC040
video:
### Explanation
Dietary terms: **vegetarian** = no meat or fish; **vegan** = no animal products at all (no meat, fish, eggs, dairy, honey); **gluten-free** = no wheat, barley or rye; **dairy-free** = no milk products; an **allergy** is a dangerous body reaction; an **intolerance** causes discomfort; **halal / kosher** follow religious rules.
### Examples
- I'm **vegan**, so no cheese on my pizza, please.
- She has a **nut allergy**.
- Is this bread **gluten-free**?
### Common mistake
- Treating vegetarian and vegan as the same; vegetarians may eat eggs and cheese, vegans do not.
### Try it
- Can a vegan eat yoghurt made from cow's milk? => No
- What is the word for food without wheat, barley or rye? => gluten-free

## KC061
video:
### Explanation
Menu descriptions list the **main ingredient**, the **cooking method** and the **sides or sauce**. Symbols such as (V) vegetarian, (VG) vegan, (GF) gluten-free help you decide quickly. To check if a dish suits someone, read every ingredient, not only the name.
### Examples
- "Garden risotto (V): rice, peas, parmesan" → vegetarian but not vegan (parmesan is cheese).
- "Chicken skewers with peanut sauce" → not suitable for a nut allergy.
- "Lentil soup (VG, GF)" → vegan and gluten-free.
### Common mistake
- Judging by the dish name alone: "Vegetable lasagne" may contain egg pasta and cheese.
### Try it
- "Mushroom burger (V): bun, cheddar, mushrooms" - is it vegan? => No, it contains cheese
- "Tomato soup (VG, GF)" - can someone who avoids gluten eat it? => Yes

## KC062
video:
### Explanation
Food labels give **allergen** information in bold or in a box ("**Contains:** milk, soy"). "**May contain** traces of nuts" warns about possible contamination. Nutrition tables show values **per 100 g** and **per serving**; check which one you are reading.
### Examples
- "Contains: **wheat**, **egg**." → not suitable for wheat or egg allergies.
- "Made in a factory that handles sesame." → risk for sesame allergy.
- "Sugar: 12 g per 100 g; 6 g per serving." → one serving is half of 100 g.
### Common mistake
- Ignoring "may contain"; for a serious allergy it matters as much as "contains".
### Try it
- "Contains: milk. May contain: peanuts." Is it safe for a peanut allergy? => No, it may contain peanuts
- A label says "Salt 1.2 g per 100 g". The pack is 200 g. How much salt? => 2.4 g

## KC063
video:
### Explanation
When you scan a restaurant review, look for opinions about **food**, **service**, **price/value** and **atmosphere**. Positive words: friendly, attentive, generous, good value. Negative words: slow, rude, overpriced, cramped. The final sentence often gives the overall rating.
### Examples
- "The staff were **attentive**, but the dishes were **overpriced**." → good service, poor value.
- "**Tiny portions** for the price." → poor value.
- "We waited **forty minutes** for a coffee." → slow service.
### Common mistake
- Taking one detail as the overall opinion; read until the end for the verdict.
### Try it
- "Delicious food, but the waiter ignored us." What was the problem? => the service
- "Huge plates at fair prices." What does the reviewer praise? => the value / portion size

## KC085
video:
### Explanation
A written dietary request should be **clear and specific**: say what you cannot eat, how serious it is (allergy or preference) and what you ask the venue to do. Use polite frames: "**I would like to let you know that**...", "**Could you please ensure that**...", "**Please could you confirm whether**...".
### Examples
- **I would like to let you know that** I have a severe shellfish allergy.
- **Could you please ensure that** my meal contains no dairy?
- **Please could you confirm whether** a vegan menu is available.
### Common mistake
- Being vague: "I don't eat some things." → name the food and the reason.
- ~~Please ensure no nuts is in food.~~ → Please ensure that **there are no nuts in** my food.
### Try it
- Make polite and specific: "No meat for me." => Could you please make sure my meal does not contain any meat? I am vegetarian.
- Complete: "Please could you ___ whether the dessert contains gelatine?" => confirm

## KC086
video:
### Explanation
Written orders (online, by message or on an order form) need the **item**, **quantity**, any **changes** and the **time or delivery details**. Use polite request forms: "**I'd like to order**...", "**Could I have**... **with / without**...", "**Please add**...".
### Examples
- **I'd like to order** two vegetable wraps for 12:30 collection.
- **Could I have** the burger **without** onions?
- **Please add** a bottle of sparkling water.
### Common mistake
- Forgetting quantity or time, so the restaurant must ask again.
- ~~One pizza, no the olives.~~ → One pizza **without** olives, please.
### Try it
- Write an order: 3 coffees, one with oat milk, pick-up at 9. => I'd like to order three coffees, one with oat milk, for pick-up at 9.
- Complete: "Could I have the salad ___ dressing on the side?" => with

## KC087
video:
### Explanation
Short dining feedback has three parts: an **overall impression**, **specific details** (a dish, the service, the price) and a **recommendation** or suggestion. Use past tenses for the visit and precise adjectives (crispy, tender, bland, attentive).
### Examples
- We had a lovely evening at Luca's.
- The fish was **tender** and the staff were very **attentive**.
- I would recommend it for a special occasion, though it is not cheap.
### Common mistake
- Only writing "Good" or "Bad"; give a reason and an example.
- ~~The food is very delicious yesterday.~~ → The food **was** delicious.
### Try it
- Add a detail to: "The dinner was nice." => The dinner was nice; the soup was rich and the bread was fresh.
- Which adjective describes food with little flavour? => bland
