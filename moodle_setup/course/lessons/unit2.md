## KC005
video:
### Explanation
A formal email opens with **Dear** + title + family name (Dear Dr Patel, Dear Mr Novak, Dear Ms Rossi). Use **Ms** when you do not know whether a woman is married. If you do not know the name at all, use a role (Dear Hiring Manager) or **Dear Sir or Madam**. Match the closing to the opening: a name → *Kind regards* or *Yours sincerely*; no name → *Yours faithfully* (British English).
### Examples
- **Dear Professor Kim**, thank you for your reply.
- **Dear Customer Service Team**, I am writing about my order.
- **Kind regards**, Lena Fischer
### Common mistake
- Using a first name with a title: ~~Dear Mr John,~~ → Dear **Mr Smith**, (or Dear John, if you know him well).
- "Hi" or "Hey" in a first formal email.
### Try it
- You write to a woman called Sara Lind whom you have never met. How do you start? => Dear Ms Lind,
- You opened with "Dear Sir or Madam". Which closing fits in British English? => Yours faithfully,

## KC006
video:
### Explanation
The present perfect (**have/has + past participle**) links past actions to now. Use it for **experience** (ever, never), for situations that continue until now (**for** a period, **since** a starting point), and for **task status** (already, yet, just). Do not use it with a finished time such as "last year".
### Examples
- I **have organised** two international conferences.
- She **has been** with the company **since** 2019.
- We **have** already **sent** the contract, but the client **hasn't signed** it **yet**.
### Common mistake
- With a finished time: ~~I have joined the firm in 2021.~~ → I **joined** the firm in 2021.
- ~~I work here since May.~~ → I **have worked** here since May.
### Try it
- Complete: "He ___ (lead) the sales team for three years." => has led
- Complete: "___ you ___ (finish) the slides yet?" => Have you finished

## KC007
video:
### Explanation
At work, direct orders can sound rude. Soften requests with modals and longer frames: **Could you...?**, **Would you...?**, **Would you mind + -ing...?**, **Would it be possible to...?**, **I was wondering if you could...**. The longer the frame, the more polite and indirect the request.
### Examples
- **Could you** forward me the client's address?
- **Would you mind** booking a room for Thursday?
- **I was wondering if you could** give me some feedback on my proposal.
### Common mistake
- ~~Would you mind to call me back?~~ → Would you mind **calling** me back?
- ~~Can you to check this?~~ → **Could you check** this?
### Try it
- Make polite: "Send me the figures." => Could you send me the figures, please?
- Complete: "Would you mind ___ (close) the window?" => closing

## KC008
video:
### Explanation
The passive (**be + past participle**) puts the focus on the action or result, not on who did it. It is common in reports: "The order **was shipped**", "The system **is being updated**", "The budget **has been approved**". Add **by + agent** only when the doer matters.
### Examples
- The new policy **was introduced** in January.
- All complaints **are answered** within 48 hours.
- The figures **have been checked** by the auditor.
### Common mistake
- Missing "be": ~~The meeting cancelled.~~ → The meeting **was cancelled**.
- Wrong participle: ~~The email was send.~~ → The email was **sent**.
### Try it
- Make passive: "Someone repaired the printer." => The printer was repaired.
- Make passive: "They review the accounts every month." => The accounts are reviewed every month.

## KC031
video:
### Explanation
Meetings have their own vocabulary. The **agenda** lists the points to discuss; the **chair** runs the meeting; the **minutes** record what was said and decided; **attendees** are the people present; **any other business (AOB)** comes last; a **consensus** is general agreement; to **adjourn** is to end or pause the meeting.
### Examples
- The first item on the **agenda** is the budget.
- Could someone take the **minutes** today?
- The meeting was **adjourned** until next week.
### Common mistake
- Confusing "minutes" (the record) with time: "Please read the minutes" means read the notes, not wait a few minutes.
### Try it
- What do we call the people present at a meeting? => attendees
- Which item usually comes at the end of the agenda? => Any other business (AOB)

## KC032
video:
### Explanation
Project language: the **scope** says what the project includes; a **deliverable** is something you must hand over; a **milestone** marks an important point; the **deadline** is the final date; a task is **on track**, **ahead of schedule** or **behind schedule**; a **stakeholder** is anyone affected by the project.
### Examples
- The prototype is our first **deliverable**.
- We reached an important **milestone** when testing started.
- The client is a key **stakeholder**.
### Common mistake
- ~~We are behind the schedule.~~ → We are **behind schedule**.
- Using "deadline" for the start date; it is always the **latest** date.
### Try it
- The project is going as planned. Which phrase fits? => on track
- What do you call anyone affected by a project? => a stakeholder

## KC052
video:
### Explanation
A good subject line tells the reader the **purpose** and often the **action** needed: "Approval needed: Q3 travel budget". Read subject lines for three things: the **topic**, the **action** (approve, reply, read) and any **deadline**. Vague subjects such as "Hello" or "Question" hide the purpose.
### Examples
- "Reminder: team photo on Monday at 9" → Information plus a time.
- "Please reply by Wed: holiday dates" → Action and deadline.
- "Update" → Unclear; the reader must open the email to know why it matters.
### Common mistake
- Judging a subject line by its length: a short one can be clear ("Room change: Meeting B") and a long one can be vague.
### Try it
- "Signature needed: new supplier contract". What must the reader do? => Sign the supplier contract
- Which is clearer: "Info" or "New parking rules from 1 June"? => New parking rules from 1 June

## KC053
video:
### Explanation
Memos are scanned, not read word for word. Look for **action words** (must, should, please, required), **who** must act (all staff, managers) and **dates or times**. Dates often signal deadlines; words like "no longer" or "from now on" signal a change.
### Examples
- "**All visitors must** sign in at reception **from Monday**." → New rule for visitors.
- "Team leaders **should submit** holiday plans **by 1 April**." → Action for team leaders with a deadline.
- "The canteen will **no longer** accept cash." → A change in payment.
### Common mistake
- Mixing up who must act: "Staff must register; managers will approve" → staff register, managers approve.
### Try it
- "IT will update all laptops on Saturday. Please leave your laptop on before you go home on Friday." What should staff do? => Leave their laptop switched on on Friday
- "From July, meeting rooms must be booked online." What has changed? => Meeting rooms are now booked online

## KC054
video:
### Explanation
Register is the level of formality. **Formal** writing uses full forms, polite frames and neutral words ("I would appreciate it if..."). **Informal** writing uses contractions, short forms, emojis, exclamation marks and casual words ("Thanks!", "gonna", "BTW"). Choose the register that fits the reader and the relationship.
### Examples
- Formal: "Please let me know whether the date is convenient."
- Informal: "Does Friday work for you?"
- Formal: "I regret to inform you that..." / Informal: "Sorry, but..."
### Common mistake
- Mixing registers in one message: ~~Dear Mr Hall, wanna meet tomorrow?~~
### Try it
- Is "Pls send asap, thx!" formal or informal? => Informal
- Make formal: "Can't come tomorrow, sorry." => I am afraid I will not be able to attend tomorrow.

## KC076
video:
### Explanation
The first sentence of a business email should state **who you are or why you write**. Common frames: "I am writing to **enquire about / request / confirm / follow up on**...", "Further to our call on Monday, ...", "I am contacting you on behalf of...". Put the main purpose in the first line.
### Examples
- **I am writing to request** a copy of my invoice.
- **Further to** our phone call, I am sending the updated timeline.
- **I am contacting you on behalf of** the Green Hotel group.
### Common mistake
- Starting with long small talk and stating the purpose only in the third paragraph.
- ~~I write you for ask about prices.~~ → I am writing **to ask about** your prices.
### Try it
- Complete the opening: "I am writing ___ (confirm) my order." => to confirm
- Which phrase follows up on an earlier conversation? => Further to our conversation,

## KC077
video:
### Explanation
When you attach a file, tell the reader what it is and why it matters. Useful phrases: "**Please find attached** the ...", "**I have attached** ... for your review", "**The attached** document shows ...". Mention the file name or content so the reader can find it.
### Examples
- **Please find attached** the signed contract.
- **I have attached** the draft schedule **for your comments**.
- **The attached** file lists all participants.
### Common mistake
- ~~Please find in attached the file.~~ → Please find **attached** the file.
- Writing "see attachment" without saying what the attachment is.
### Try it
- Complete: "Please find ___ my updated CV." => attached
- Make it more specific: "See the attachment." => The attached report shows our results for April.

## KC078
video:
### Explanation
Action items are short lines that tell someone what to do. Start each with an **imperative verb** (Send, Book, Confirm, Review), add the **object** and, if needed, the **owner** and **deadline**. Keep all items in the **same grammatical form** (parallel structure).
### Examples
- **Book** the training room for 3 May. (Maria)
- **Confirm** the speaker list by Friday.
- **Share** the survey results with the team.
### Common mistake
- Mixing forms: ~~Sending the invoice; Call the bank; the report should be checked~~ → **Send** the invoice; **Call** the bank; **Check** the report.
- Writing full paragraphs instead of one action per line.
### Try it
- Rewrite as an action item: "It would be good if someone updated the website." => Update the website.
- Make parallel: "Order paper; Printer repair; Email Tom" => Order paper; Repair the printer; Email Tom
