## KC001
video:
### Explanation
In the present simple, a verb after **he, she, it** or one person or thing (my brother, the bus) takes an ending. Most verbs add **-s** (work → works). Verbs ending in -s, -sh, -ch, -x or -o add **-es** (wash → washes, go → goes). A consonant + y changes to **-ies** (carry → carries).
### Examples
- My father **leaves** home at seven.
- The baby **cries** when she is hungry.
- Our teacher **fixes** the printer every Monday.
### Common mistake
- Forgetting the ending: ~~She drive to work.~~ → She **drives** to work.
- Adding -s after I, you, we, they: ~~They plays tennis.~~ → They **play** tennis.
### Try it
- Complete: "Tom ___ (finish) work at five." => finishes
- Complete: "The cat ___ (sleep) on the sofa." => sleeps
- Complete: "Lucy ___ (study) at night." => studies

## KC002
video:
### Explanation
To make a present simple sentence negative, put **don't** (do not) or **doesn't** (does not) before the main verb. Use **doesn't** with he, she, it; use **don't** with I, you, we, they. After don't/doesn't the main verb has **no -s**.
### Examples
- I **don't eat** meat.
- My sister **doesn't like** early mornings.
- The shop **doesn't open** on Sundays.
### Common mistake
- Keeping the -s after doesn't: ~~He doesn't works late.~~ → He **doesn't work** late.
- Using "not" alone: ~~We not watch TV.~~ → We **don't watch** TV.
### Try it
- Make negative: "Ben plays the guitar." => Ben doesn't play the guitar.
- Make negative: "They walk to school." => They don't walk to school.

## KC003
video:
### Explanation
Yes/No questions about habits start with **Do** or **Does**. Use **Does** with he, she, it and **Do** with I, you, we, they. The main verb stays in its base form, and the short answer repeats the auxiliary: "Yes, she does." / "No, they don't."
### Examples
- **Do** you **cook** dinner every evening? - Yes, I do.
- **Does** your father **work** at weekends? - No, he doesn't.
- **Do** the children **wear** a uniform? - Yes, they do.
### Common mistake
- Adding -s to the main verb: ~~Does he plays chess?~~ → Does he **play** chess?
- Using "is" for habits: ~~Is she drink tea?~~ → **Does** she drink tea?
### Try it
- Make a question: "Anna reads the news every day." => Does Anna read the news every day?
- Give a short answer: "Do your friends live nearby?" (yes) => Yes, they do.

## KC004
video:
### Explanation
Adverbs of frequency (always, usually, often, sometimes, rarely, never) say how often something happens. They go **before the main verb** but **after the verb be**. With an auxiliary (don't, can), they go after the auxiliary.
### Examples
- I **usually** have toast for breakfast.
- She is **always** on time.
- We don't **often** eat out.
### Common mistake
- Putting the adverb after the main verb: ~~He drinks always coffee.~~ → He **always drinks** coffee.
- Putting it before "be": ~~They never are tired.~~ → They are **never** tired.
### Try it
- Add "sometimes": "I take a taxi home." => I sometimes take a taxi home.
- Add "usually": "The bus is late." => The bus is usually late.

## KC029
video:
### Explanation
Morning routines use fixed verb + noun pairs. We **brush** our teeth, **wash** our face, **comb** or **brush** our hair, **shave**, **take** (or have) a shower, **get dressed** and **make** the bed. Learning them as pairs helps you choose the right verb quickly.
### Examples
- I **wash my face** with cold water.
- He **gets dressed** after breakfast.
- She **makes her bed** before she leaves.
### Common mistake
- "Do the bed" → **make** the bed.
- "Make a shower" → **take** (or have) a shower.
### Try it
- Which verb? "I ___ dressed in five minutes." => get
- Which verb? "Please ___ your bed before school." => make

## KC030
video:
### Explanation
Transport verbs depend on the vehicle. You **catch** or **take** a bus or train, **ride** a bike or motorbike, **drive** a car, and **get on / get off** public transport. To **commute** means to travel regularly between home and work or school.
### Examples
- I **catch** the 8:10 train every morning.
- My brother **rides** his scooter to college.
- We **get off** the bus at the third stop.
### Common mistake
- ~~I drive my bike.~~ → I **ride** my bike.
- ~~Go out the bus here.~~ → **Get off** the bus here.
### Try it
- Which verb? "She ___ her car to the office." => drives
- Which verb? "Hurry, or we won't ___ the last bus!" => catch

## KC049
video:
### Explanation
Daily planners and notes are usually written in time order. To find the sequence, look at the **times** (7:00, 7:30) and at **order words** such as first, then, after that, before and later. Read the whole text before answering "what happens next?".
### Examples
- "7:00 run. 7:40 shower. 8:00 breakfast." → The shower comes between the run and breakfast.
- "Before work I walk the dog." → Walking the dog happens first.
- "Later, I meet Sam for lunch." → Lunch comes after the earlier activities.
### Common mistake
- Following the order of the sentences instead of the times: "I have lunch at 1. At 11 I visit the bank." → The bank visit is **first**.
### Try it
- "Wake up 6:45, gym 7:15, work 9:00." What comes right after waking up? => The gym
- "After dinner I wash the dishes. Then I read." What happens last? => Reading

## KC050
video:
### Explanation
Schedules list facts in a compact way: **day, time, activity, place**. When a question asks "when" or "where", find the line for the activity first, then read only the time or place on that line. A range such as 14:00-15:30 tells you the duration.
### Examples
- "Wed 10:00 Haircut, Lane Street" → The haircut is at 10:00 on Lane Street.
- "Sat 09:00-11:00 Football, North Park" → Football lasts two hours.
- "Sun 16:30 Call Grandma" → No place is given; only a time.
### Common mistake
- Reading the time from the line above or below. Always check the activity and the time are on the **same line**.
### Try it
- "Thu 13:15 Lunch with Ana, Café Rio." Where is lunch? => Café Rio
- "Mon 08:00-08:45 Swimming." How long is swimming? => 45 minutes

## KC051
video:
### Explanation
Inference means understanding something the text does not say directly. In a weekly timetable, an activity that **repeats** (every Monday, daily, three times a week) shows a habit. Ask: what kind of person would have this routine?
### Examples
- "Mon-Fri 6:00 yoga" → The person exercises early and regularly.
- "Every Saturday: library 10-12" → The person probably enjoys reading or studying.
- "Daily 20:00 call family" → Family contact is important to them.
### Common mistake
- Inferring too much: "Tue 19:00 piano" does **not** prove the person is a professional pianist; it shows a regular hobby.
### Try it
- "Mon, Wed, Fri 18:00 language class." What can you infer? => The person is learning a language regularly.
- "Every night 23:30 bed, 9:30 wake up." What habit does this show? => Going to bed and getting up late.

## KC073
video:
### Explanation
In writing, the verb must agree with its subject. A **singular** subject (my friend, everyone, the team) takes a singular verb (walks, is); a **plural** subject (my friends, the students) takes a plural verb (walk, are). Two subjects joined by **and** are plural.
### Examples
- My neighbour **feeds** the birds every morning.
- My neighbours **feed** the birds every morning.
- Paul and Rita **share** a car.
### Common mistake
- Matching the verb to the nearest noun: ~~The box of pens are empty.~~ → The box of pens **is** empty.
- ~~Everybody have a key.~~ → Everybody **has** a key.
### Try it
- Correct the sentence: "The students in my class lives nearby." => The students in my class live nearby.
- Correct the sentence: "Each of the rooms have a window." => Each of the rooms has a window.

## KC074
video:
### Explanation
When you write about routines, place frequency words carefully: **before the main verb**, **after be**, and **after an auxiliary**. Longer expressions such as every day, once a week or on Fridays usually go at the **end** of the sentence (or at the start for emphasis).
### Examples
- I **rarely** cook during the week, but I cook a big meal **on Sundays**.
- My bus is **often** crowded.
- We go swimming **twice a week**.
### Common mistake
- ~~I every day check my email.~~ → I check my email **every day**.
- ~~She goes usually by train.~~ → She **usually goes** by train.
### Try it
- Fix the order: "He plays every week football." => He plays football every week.
- Add "always": "I forget my keys." => I always forget my keys.

## KC075
video:
### Explanation
Sequence connectors guide the reader through a routine: **First**, **Then**, **Next**, **After that**, **Finally**. Put them at the start of the sentence and follow most of them with a comma. Use "Finally" only for the last step.
### Examples
- **First**, I switch off my alarm. **Then** I open the curtains.
- **After that**, I make a cup of tea.
- **Finally**, I check my bag and leave.
### Common mistake
- Starting with "Finally" for an early step, or using "First" twice in one paragraph.
- ~~After that I, eat~~ → **After that, I eat** breakfast.
### Try it
- Choose the connector for the last step: "___, I lock the door." => Finally
- Order these: "Then I dry my hair. / First, I wash my hair." => First, I wash my hair. Then I dry my hair.
