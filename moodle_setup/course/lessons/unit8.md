## KC025
video:
### Explanation
Health instructions use the **imperative**: base verb, no subject (Rest, Drink, Take, Avoid). Add **Don't** for things to avoid. Time and frequency phrases make the instruction precise: **twice a day**, **after meals**, **for three days**.
### Examples
- **Rest** for at least two days.
- **Take** one tablet after breakfast.
- **Don't** lift anything heavy this week.
### Common mistake
- ~~You must to rest.~~ → **Rest.** (or You must rest.)
- ~~No eat before the test.~~ → **Don't eat** before the test.
### Try it
- Make an instruction: "you / apply / cream / twice a day" => Apply the cream twice a day.
- Make a negative instruction: "you / drink alcohol / with this medicine" => Don't drink alcohol with this medicine.

## KC026
video:
### Explanation
**Should** gives advice: "You **should** see a doctor." **Had better** ('d better) gives strong, urgent advice with a warning of a bad result: "You'**d better** stay at home, or you'll get worse." Both are followed by the **base verb**; the negatives are **shouldn't** and **had better not**.
### Examples
- You **should** drink more water.
- You **shouldn't** skip breakfast.
- You**'d better** go to A&E - that cut looks deep.
### Common mistake
- ~~You had better to rest.~~ → You **had better rest**.
- ~~You hadn't better go.~~ → You **had better not** go.
### Try it
- Give advice: "go to bed earlier" => You should go to bed earlier.
- Strong negative advice: "eat that old chicken" => You'd better not eat that old chicken.

## KC027
video:
### Explanation
The first conditional links a real possible situation to its likely result: **If + present simple, will + verb**. In health contexts it connects symptoms and actions: "If the fever **goes** up, **call** the doctor" (an imperative can replace will). The if-clause can come first (with a comma) or second.
### Examples
- **If** you **rest**, you **will feel** better soon.
- **If** the pain **continues**, **book** an appointment.
- You **will sleep** better **if** you **avoid** screens at night.
### Common mistake
- ~~If you will take the medicine, you feel better.~~ → If you **take** the medicine, you **will feel** better.
### Try it
- Complete: "If your throat ___ (hurt), drink warm tea." => hurts
- Complete: "If you exercise regularly, you ___ (have) more energy." => will have

## KC028
video:
### Explanation
Dosage instructions use exact quantities: **one / two tablets**, **5 ml** (a teaspoon), **a glass of** water, **every four hours**, **no more than** four doses in 24 hours. Liquids are uncountable (**some / much / a little** water), tablets are countable (**a few / many** tablets).
### Examples
- Take **two tablets every six hours**.
- Drink **at least two litres** of water a day.
- Do not take **more than 20 ml** in one day.
### Common mistake
- ~~How many water should I drink?~~ → How **much** water...
- ~~Take one tablets.~~ → Take **one tablet** / **two tablets**.
### Try it
- Complete: "Take ___ (5 ml) of syrup at night." => 5 ml / a teaspoon
- Complete: "How ___ tablets can I take in a day?" => many

## KC047
video:
### Explanation
Symptom words: a **headache** (pain in the head), a **sore throat**, a **cough**, a **fever / temperature** (high body heat), **nausea** (feeling you may vomit), **dizziness** (feeling the room is spinning), a **rash** (red spots on the skin), **a runny / blocked nose**.
### Examples
- I've got a **sore throat** and a **cough**.
- She felt **dizzy** when she stood up.
- The child has a **rash** on her arms.
### Common mistake
- ~~I have a pain of head.~~ → I have a **headache**.
- ~~I have fever.~~ → I have **a fever** / **a temperature**.
### Try it
- What do you call red spots on the skin? => a rash
- Which word means feeling you might be sick? => nausea

## KC048
video:
### Explanation
Pharmacy and medical words: a **thermometer** measures body temperature; a **bandage** covers a wound; a **plaster** (US: Band-Aid) covers a small cut; a **prescription** is a doctor's written order for medicine; **over-the-counter** medicine needs no prescription; a **pharmacist** prepares and sells medicine; **painkillers** reduce pain.
### Examples
- The doctor gave me a **prescription** for antibiotics.
- You can buy **painkillers over the counter**.
- Put a **plaster** on that cut.
### Common mistake
- Confusing **pharmacist** (the person) with **pharmacy** (the shop).
### Try it
- What do you use to measure a temperature? => a thermometer
- What is medicine you can buy without a prescription called? => over-the-counter medicine

## KC070
video:
### Explanation
Prescription labels tell you **how much** to take, **how often**, **when** (with food, before bed) and **for how long**. Abbreviations: **tab** = tablet, **ml** = millilitre, **daily** = once a day, **twice daily** = two times a day. "**Complete the course**" means finish all the tablets.
### Examples
- "1 tab twice daily with food for 7 days" → 2 tablets a day, with meals, for a week.
- "Take 30 minutes **before** breakfast" → on an empty stomach.
- "Do not exceed 4 doses in 24 hours." → maximum four times a day.
### Common mistake
- Stopping when you feel better although the label says "complete the course".
### Try it
- "2 tabs three times daily." How many tablets per day? => six
- "Take at bedtime." When do you take it? => just before going to bed

## KC071
video:
### Explanation
Warning labels list **side effects** (possible unwanted effects: drowsiness, nausea) and **precautions** (things to avoid or check). Key phrases: "**May cause** drowsiness" (it can make you sleepy), "**Do not** drive or operate machinery", "**Consult** your doctor **if**...", "**Keep out of reach** of children".
### Examples
- "**May cause drowsiness.** Do not drive." → do not drive after taking it.
- "**Avoid** alcohol." → no alcohol with this medicine.
- "**Consult** a doctor **if** pregnant." → ask a doctor first if pregnant.
### Common mistake
- Reading "may cause" as "will cause"; it is a possible effect, but you must still follow the precaution.
### Try it
- "May cause drowsiness. Do not operate machinery." Should you use a power drill after taking it? => No
- "Keep out of reach of children." Where should you store it? => Somewhere children cannot reach

## KC072
video:
### Explanation
Wellness articles mix **background** with **advice**. Advice is signalled by imperatives (Try, Avoid), modals (should, can help) and phrases like **experts recommend** or **it is a good idea to**. To scan for recommendations, look for these signals rather than reading every statistic.
### Examples
- "**Experts recommend** seven to nine hours of sleep." → recommendation.
- "**Try** to stand up every 30 minutes." → recommendation.
- "Back pain affects many office workers." → background, not advice.
### Common mistake
- Taking a background fact as advice ("Many people skip breakfast" does not tell you to skip it).
### Try it
- "Stretching before bed can help you relax." What is recommended? => Stretching before bed
- "One in three adults sleeps less than six hours." Is this advice? => No, it is background information

## KC094
video:
### Explanation
On an intake form, describe each symptom with **what**, **where**, **since when**, **how strong** and **what makes it better or worse**. Use the present perfect or **since / for**: "I **have had** a cough **for** a week." Be specific; doctors cannot use "I feel bad".
### Examples
- I **have had** pain in my right knee **since** Saturday.
- It is worse when I climb stairs.
- The pain is about **6 out of 10**.
### Common mistake
- ~~I have pain since two days.~~ → I **have had** pain **for** two days.
- Vague: "Something is wrong with my stomach." → say where, how long and how it feels.
### Try it
- Write: dizziness, started three days ago. => I have felt dizzy for three days.
- Add a detail: "My shoulder hurts." => My left shoulder hurts when I lift my arm.

## KC095
video:
### Explanation
A wellness plan lists **daily actions** with **times or amounts**. Use advisory forms: **I will**, **I should**, **Try to**, **It is a good idea to**, plus precise details (8 glasses, 20 minutes, before 11 pm). Short bullet points in time order are easiest to follow.
### Examples
- 07:00 - **Drink** a glass of water before coffee.
- **Try to** walk for 20 minutes at lunchtime.
- **It is a good idea to** switch off screens by 22:30.
### Common mistake
- Vague goals: "Be healthier." → give an action, an amount and a time.
- ~~Try walk~~ → **Try to walk**.
### Try it
- Make specific: "Drink more water." => Drink a glass of water every two hours.
- Complete: "It is a good idea ___ (stretch) after a long drive." => to stretch

## KC096
video:
### Explanation
A formal appointment request includes **who you are**, **what you need** (a consultation, a check-up), **when you are available** and **how to contact you**. Useful frames: "**I would like to book / arrange** an appointment...", "**I am available**...", "**Please let me know** if...".
### Examples
- **I would like to arrange** a check-up with Dr Okafor.
- **I am available** on weekday afternoons after 3 pm.
- **Please let me know** whether a video consultation is possible.
### Common mistake
- Missing availability, so the clinic has to write back.
- Too direct: "I need an appointment tomorrow." → **I would like to book** an appointment for tomorrow, **if possible**.
### Try it
- Write an opening: dental check-up. => I would like to book a dental check-up.
- State availability: Monday or Wednesday mornings. => I am available on Monday or Wednesday mornings.
